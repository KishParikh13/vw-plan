How we talk to young people

Not "ancient scripture." A clear way to steady a busy, anxious mind.

We lead with the problems young people feel every day, stress, comparison, burnout, "what am I doing with my life", and let Vedanta's answer be the pleasant surprise. Dr. Janki's calm, logical teaching is the trust. Her face and voice come first, always.

The path, start to finish

From a 30-second video to the one-year program

Nobody joins a year-long program after one video. We build trust in steps. Each step earns the next.

Step 3

Step 4

Start small, on purpose

Version one runs on one part-time volunteer, about 8 to 10 hours a week. Their only job: turn existing lectures into a few short videos each week and collect emails. Everything else waits until this is working.

Grow only when it's earned

Each new helper or small budget unlocks the next stage: email nurturing, then paid courses, then live sessions and the program itself. We never get ahead of what's proven.

Two audiences, two approaches

We are reaching two different groups at once

They have different worries, live in different places online, and need different things from us. The plan serves both without splitting our effort.

18 to 25 — the seekers

Where they are: TikTok, Instagram Reels, YouTube Shorts.

What they feel: anxiety, "what am I doing with my life," comparison, stress about the future. They are open but skeptical.

What works: short punchy clips that name a real feeling in the first two seconds. They share things that make them feel understood. They discover new voices on TikTok first.

Path for them: Clip → follow → free talks → book → Foundation Course over 6-12 months.

35 to 45 — the ready ones

Where they are: LinkedIn, podcasts, word of mouth from trusted communities.

What they feel: they have the career, the family. Something still feels off. They are ready to go deeper, not just scroll.

What works: Dr. Janki's intellectual rigor. "Applied philosophy" framing. A podcast appearance or a talk in their city lands harder than a Reel.

Path for them: Podcast / LinkedIn post → website → intro call → Foundation Course or direct Diploma inquiry. Faster conversion, larger gift size.

Why this matters for resources: short-form clips serve the 18-25 group and also reach some 35-45s. So we do clips first. Podcast outreach and LinkedIn content come when we have a second volunteer.
What success looks like in 90 days: a steady habit of a few short videos a week, 500 to 1,500 people on our email list, and the first US student in a paid course. Not big money yet, proof the path works.

Why the reach fell

Four fixable reasons

  • 1
    The first two seconds don't grab. Recent videos open with slides and text instead of a person and a hook. People scroll past.
  • 2
    Only on YouTube. Young people discover new voices on Instagram Reels and TikTok. The short videos aren't being shared there.
  • 3
    Made for India, not the US. The content assumes a Mumbai schedule and context. It needs a light US translation.
  • 4
    Nowhere to go next. A viewer who wants more is sent to a WhatsApp number in India. There is no US email sign-up, no easy next step.

Donations

How people come to give

We studied how respected groups (Self-Realization Fellowship, Chinmaya Mission, Isha) actually raise money. The lesson is the same everywhere: give value first, ask later, and make the ask specific.

Cold

Give freely

Free videos. No ask at all. Just teaching.

Warm

Invite in

They join the email community. Trust grows.

Ready

Ask, specifically

"Help a student attend the Foundation Course." Named, not vague.

Loyal

Monthly giving

A named group of monthly supporters who keep the teaching free.

Use Zeffy to collect donations

It is completely free for nonprofits. No platform fee, no card fee. A $10 gift arrives as $10. Other tools (PayPal, Stripe) quietly take 5 to 7 percent of small gifts. Zeffy is used by over 100,000 nonprofits.

Name the gift, don't beg

"Sponsor a student" beats "please donate." People give when they can picture exactly what their money does. Lead in plain English; introduce the idea of dana (sacred giving) over time.

Finding people

Where to recruit volunteers

We don't need many. We need one or two who care. Here is where they actually are.

Seva networks

Sewa International (30+ US chapters), Chinmaya Mission volunteers, BAPS. People here already give their time to dharmic causes.

University groups

Hindu Student Council — 60+ campus chapters, 200,000+ students over time (MIT, NYU, Michigan, Rutgers). Perfect age for clip-making.

Online communities

r/hinduism (63K members), r/vedanta, and Advaita/Vedanta Discord servers. Both a place to find help and to share content.

How to structure it: copy what works, clear roles like Chinmaya, flexible time like Isha. Offer a simple 3-month trial so young volunteers aren't scared off by a big commitment.
